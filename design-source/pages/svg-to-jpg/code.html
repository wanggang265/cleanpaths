Why convert SVG to JPG?

SVG is ideal for keeping artwork scalable — but the world of documents, print shops and photo workflows runs on JPG. A logo that looks razor-sharp in a design tool can be refused outright by a print order form, an email signature, or the image field of a CMS. Converting renders your SVG into a standard JPEG at the size you need, right in your browser: no upload step, and the preview shows the result before you download.

Read this if your SVG has a transparent background

JPG cannot store transparency — that's a property of the format itself, not a limitation of this tool. When your SVG floats on transparency, the converted JPG has to fill that area with a solid color. What fills it is an implementation detail this copy doesn't lock down; the preview shows you exactly what you'll get before anything downloads. If the transparent background is the point — for a logo or icon you'll place over other content — use our SVG to PNG converter instead. PNG keeps the alpha channel; JPG never will.

SVG to JPG or SVG to PNG — which one?

Pick JPG when the destination is print, a photo workflow, a document, an email, or a form with an upload cap — anywhere that lives on photographs and expects small files. Pick PNG when transparency matters, or when the artwork has sharp text and edges you'll keep editing. Still unsure? Our SVG to PNG converter is the safer default for graphics; come back here when the destination turns out to be a print form. Both run on your device, so checking both costs you nothing.

Where a JPG beats the original SVG

Print order forms and photo labs: JPG is their native tongue, and many reject vector files at the door. Office documents and slide decks: a JPG embeds and travels with the file — no missing-font surprises, no app-dependent rendering. Email signatures and messaging apps, where an SVG often shows up as a broken icon or a downloadable attachment. And any upload form with a size limit: a JPG render of your artwork is usually far smaller than a PNG at the same dimensions.

FAQ
What happens to my SVG's transparent background?
It gets filled, because JPG has no alpha channel. The preview shows you exactly what the filled result looks like before you download, so check it there. If the transparency is important to you, don't settle for the fill — our SVG to PNG converter keeps the alpha channel intact.
What size will the JPG be?
A vector file has no fixed pixel size, so the tool offers output size options when you convert. For print, render larger — a bigger render gives the printer more detail to work with. For email and documents, a moderate size keeps the file light. The preview shows what the current choice produces before you download.
Is JPG or PNG better for my logo?
PNG, for most logo jobs: it keeps the transparent background and the razor-sharp edges that logos live on. JPG earns its place when the destination demands it — print order forms, documents, email signatures — or when file size is the constraint. If you can only keep one, keep the PNG; it converts to JPG later without losing anything that matters for a graphic.
Do you upload or store my files?
No. Conversion runs entirely in your browser — there is no upload step. We never receive, see, or store your images or the files you generate. Close the tab and they're gone; we never had them. This is how the tool is built, not a marketing claim. Details in our Privacy Policy.
